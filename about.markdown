Hi, I’m **Ki-Hoon Lee** — and welcome to my portfolio site.

I'm a mechanical engineer passionate about designing products and technologies that improve lives. My main interests lie in **energy technology** and **product design** — a direction shaped by my visits to underserved communities and refugee camps, where I witnessed firsthand the deep challenges caused by energy scarcity, and the need for solutions that are human-centered, reliable, and scalable.

### 🌏 A Global Perspective

I was born in 🇹🇭 **Thailand**, raised in 🇰🇷 **Korea**, 🇨🇭 **Switzerland**, 🇳🇵 **Nepal**, and later 🇬🇧 **the UK** for my undergraduate degree. ### 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Inspired by Legacy

My grandfather, who rose from hardship after the Korean War to become a respected diplomat, and my mother, who spent her life working with the UNHCR across continents, instilled in me the value of **humanitarian impact over personal gain**. From them, I learned that engineering is not just about equations — it’s about solving problems that improve lives.

---

### 🪖 My Turning Point

During my 18-month military service, I volunteered for a forward post just 2 km from the North Korean border. Despite language barriers, harsh conditions, and 24/7 alertness, I grew as a leader and teammate. That experience sharpened my focus and gave me the resilience I carry into every challenge — personal or technical.

---

### 🛠 Why I Build

Whether it’s a **resistive rehabilitation treadmill**, a **solar-powered UAV simulator**, or a **machine learning classifier** for health and sports analytics, I design systems with real-world users in mind.  

What excites me most is **product design** — taking an idea from concept to sketch to physical prototype. I enjoy diving deep into mechanical layouts, failure points, manufacturability, and user interaction. At UC Berkeley, I led the prototyping of an **omnidirectional treadmill** that combined magnetic and friction braking in a modular design — cutting costs by 40% while improving usability for rehabilitation patients.

I find joy in making things that move, respond, and work reliably in the real world — especially when they’re designed for people who don’t always get thoughtful solutions built for them.

> — But I believe we’re here at just the right time to change the world.
